Shooting

  • Crosswind drifts the bullet. It stays light until fifteen rats are down, then gusts can push a 60 m shot up to 2 mil. Aim into the wind; the arrow on the gauge shows where it blows across your view.
  • The scope is zeroed at 50 m. Closer in the bullet lands up to 3 cm high, and at 60 m about 3 cm low. The ticks under the crosshair are 1 mil each.
  • Rats have personalities: sentinels raise the alarm, veterans zig-zag, swimmers dive, skulkers freeze.
  • After every shot you work the bolt (about 1.6 s). The next round isn't chambered until it locks, and the rifle needs re-aiming.
  • Thermal shows warm bodies anywhere; digital and tube modes need the IR beam to light a rat's eyes.
